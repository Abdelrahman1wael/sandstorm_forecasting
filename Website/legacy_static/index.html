Complete Research Proposal Breakdown

Structured according to the University of Science and Technology Beijing (北京科技大学) Master's Thesis Topic Selection standards, covering background, literature evolution, methodology, steps, and timeline.

1.1 Research Background & Problem Formulation

Against the backdrop of global climate change, extreme sandstorms pose severe transnational threats. Research indicates that over 330 million people across 151 countries are directly affected. In China, arid and semi-arid regions in the north act as principal sources, where dust plumes transport across continents to central, eastern, and southern China.

Between 2010 and 2013 alone, direct economic losses reached ~US$1.0 billion in northern China. Impacts include catastrophic visibility drops endangering aviation and highway transit, severe PM10/PM2.5 inhalation triggering respiratory and cardiovascular illnesses, and soil desertification.

Traditional NWP Bottleneck: Supercomputers take hours for single high-res run; physical parameterization errors accumulate exponentially; skill drops steeply for extended-range (3–15 days) and seasonal forecasts.

The emergence of data-driven machine learning, exemplified by China's independently developed AI-GAMFS large model (5km resolution, 2x daily update, 38%–74% error reduction vs ECMWF & NASA), proves that AI can capture complex non-linear dynamics and extend lead times to 120 hours (5 days).

1.2 Theoretical & Practical Significance

Theoretical Significance:
  • Cross-Scale Driving Mechanisms: Quantifying how sub-seasonal to seasonal forcing (ENSO, Arctic Oscillation, blocking highs, snowmelt, vegetation phenology) modulates synoptic cold fronts.
  • New Algorithmic Paradigms: Advancing spatiotemporal sequence modeling via LSTM, Transformers, and Graph Neural Networks (GNN) combined with Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PINN mass conservation).
  • Unified Benchmark: Establishing standardized open datasets and 3–15 day forecast labels with rigorous meteorological verification.
Practical Significance:
  • Proactive Defense: Transitioning from "passive post-event response" to "proactive outlooks" (watering, dust suppression, healthcare logistics).
  • Sector Meteorological Support: Optimizing aviation flight plans, protecting photovoltaic solar energy panels from soiling, and reinforcing agricultural greenhouses.
  • Ecological Civilization: Distinguishing natural desert dust from anthropogenic industrial emissions, guiding the "Three-North" Shelter Forest Program (三北防护林).
  • International Competitiveness: Elevating national leadership in environmental meteorological AI systems.

2.1 Evolution of Sandstorm AI Forecasting

Early ANN & SVM Explorations: Huang et al. [3] (2006) introduced stepwise regression + multi-layer perceptron across 4 stations. Lu et al. [4] (2006) utilized SVM with 500 hPa geopotential height and wind fields, highlighting severe class imbalance issues.

Imbalance Handling & Ensembles: Xie et al. [5] (2015) designed hybrid adaptive sampling. Zhang et al. [6] (2015) coupled SMOTE with AdaBoost random forest. Kaboodvandpour et al. [7] (2015) compared ANN, SVM, and gradient boosting with PM10 data.

Deep Learning & Hybrid Models: Li et al. [10] (2019) proposed INB-CNN fusing spatial CNN with Naive Bayes for land cover. Harba et al. [11] (2020) used Feature Pyramid Networks on satellite imagery. Ren et al. [12] (2020) demonstrated transfer learning, and Ren et al. [16] (2022) applied Stacking ensembles fusing LSTM and CNN.

2.2 Weather Machine Learning in Parallel Domains

Disastrous Weather: Song (2024) [25, 26] achieved 96.72% hit rate in thunderstorm gales via AutoGluon and AutoFeat-TPOT for fog [27]. Zhao et al. (2025) [29] built AeolusStorm + Random Forest for rocket launch safety.

Key Physical Quantities: Bai et al. (2024) [35] modeled Zenith Tropospheric Delay (ZTD). Bai et al. (2025) [36] estimated Boundary Layer Height (PBLH) with XGBoost. Liu (2022) [38] created EALSTM-QR for wind interval prediction. Google NeuralGCM [40] demonstrated hybrid planetary modeling.

Cross-Industry Applications: Power load forecasting (Zhao 2025 [41], Zheng 2023 [42]), wind power (Zhang 2024 [43]), rail passenger flow via GCNN+Transformer (Chen 2024 [50]), and agricultural yield models across Pakistan [53], Rwanda [56], and Thailand [39].

2.3 Research Gaps & Proposed Innovation

Identified Scientific Gaps:
  • Prevailing research restricted to short-term/nowcasting; medium- to long-term (3–15d) ignored.
  • Over-reliance on single station data; lack of multi-source heterogeneous fusion (satellite + NWP + ERA5).
  • Absence of spatiotemporal GNN and Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) architectures.
  • Lack of unified benchmark datasets and physical interpretability.

This Study's Target: Bridge these gaps by constructing a multi-source spatiotemporal tensor system, a dual-line machine learning framework, and embedding mass conservation laws.

(1) Sandstorm Data System Construction

  • Target Scales: 3–15 days medium-to-long term forecasts + monthly/seasonal trend outlooks.
  • Multi-Source Acquisition: Numerical forecast ensembles (ECMWF, CMA), climate reanalysis (ERA5), satellite remote sensing (MODIS, Himawari, FY-4), and surface environmental sensor arrays.
  • Spatiotemporal Alignment: Regridding onto unified 0.125° grid, spatial interpolation, missing data imputation via Kriging, and multi-level z-score standardization.
  • Sample Labeling: Categorical event occurrence (0/1), 4-tier intensity levels (floating dust, blowing sand, sandstorm, severe storm), and spatial polygon boundaries.

(2) Dual Machine Learning Forecasting Pathways

  • Main Line A: NWP Statistical Post-Processing: Utilizing multi-model numerical outputs as primary inputs, training Random Forest, XGBoost, and EALSTM-QR models for systematic bias correction and probabilistic quantile estimation.
  • Main Line B: Multi-Source End-to-End Deep Learning: Formulating spatiotemporal sequence models (LSTM, Earthformer, PredRNN, GNN) mapping environmental tensors directly to sandstorm risk probabilities.
  • Class Imbalance Remedies: Integrating SMOTE oversampling, cost-sensitive loss penalties, and focal loss to guarantee high recall on rare extreme events.

(3) Model Validation & Interpretability

  • Multi-Dimensional Validation: Categorical verification (POD, FAR, CSI), regression accuracy (RMSE, MAE, R²), and probabilistic discrimination (AUC-ROC, Brier score).
  • Retrospective Case Studies: Diagnosing performance across landmark historical events (e.g. April 2025 Sichuan remote intrusion).
  • Mechanistic Transparency: Utilizing SHAP values and cross-attention weight heatmaps to verify compliance with aerodynamic and thermodynamic principles.
  • Operational Evaluation: Lead-time performance decay curves and inference stability on high-resolution 5km grids.

Method 1: Critical Literature Review

Systematic synthesis of domestic and international literature spanning sandstorm numerical simulations, classical statistical forecasts, and frontier deep learning. Avoids redundant efforts and pinpoints the unexplored 3–15 day extended-range predictability horizon.

Method 2: Physics-Informed Machine Learning

Bypasses rigid numerical parameterization while preserving physical realism by incorporating aerodynamic thresholds ($u_* > u_{*t}$) and atmospheric mass conservation laws directly into the neural loss function:

L_total = L_data + λ_1 • L_mass_conservation + λ_2 • L_threshold(u* − u*t)

Captures complex nonlinear couplings between land surface phenology, synoptic cold vortices, and aerosol saltation.

Method 3: Case Study & Mechanistic Diagnostics

Transcends macro-statistical metrics by conducting deep retrospective diagnoses of 1–2 typical severe dust storm processes. Compares machine learning spatial forecasts against operational NWP baselines, visualizing attention weights across upstream source regions.

Step 1: Multi-Source Data Collection & Standardized Preprocessing

Acquisition of ECMWF, CMA, ERA5, MODIS, and 2,400+ CMA station records → Quality control → Spatiotemporal regridding onto 0.125° grid → Kriging missing value imputation → Z-score tensor normalization → 3-tier label generation.

Step 2: Feature Engineering & Input Representation

Construction of three core feature tensors: (a) Dust Emission Tensor: Friction wind speed $u_*$, volumetric soil moisture (0–7cm), bare soil fraction, seasonal snow cover, roughness length $z_0$. (b) Dynamic Transport Tensor: 10m wind velocity vectors, vertical shear, 500hPa geopotential height, boundary layer height PBLH, cold front advection pathways. (c) Upstream Memory Tensor: Historical 72h dust concentration accumulation, spatial neighborhood graph adjacency matrix.

Step 3: Dual Model Construction & Training

Main Line A (NWP Bias Correction via RF, XGBoost, EALSTM-QR) + Main Line B (End-to-End Spatiotemporal Sequence Modeling via LSTM, Earthformer, GNN, PINN).

Step 4: Probability Calibration & Rare Event Optimization

Applying SMOTE synthetic oversampling on severe dust storm days + Cost-sensitive focal loss to heavily penalize disaster misses + Platt scaling probability calibration.

Step 5: Model Evaluation, Retrospective Review & Operational Product Generation

Validation across POD, FAR, CSI, AUC, RMSE → Comparison against baseline NWP models → Case review of April 2025 Sichuan intrusion → SHAP interpretability → Generation of 5km risk probability contours.

Artificial Intelligence Architecture

The Role of Artificial Intelligence in Sandstorm Prediction

How modern machine learning algorithms break computational bottlenecks, extract high-dimensional nonlinear patterns, and enforce physical conservation principles.

🌟 AI-GAMFS Coupled Large Model

Developed by a premier Chinese research team, AI-GAMFS is the world's first aerosol-meteorology coupled forecasting large model. It operates at 5-kilometer spatial resolution with twice-daily operational cycles.

Reduces East Asia forecast errors by 38% to 74% compared to ECMWF and NASA GEOS-CF. Demonstrated a landmark 120-hour (5-day) advance warning of severe northern dust invading the Sichuan Basin in April 2025.

⚛️ Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN)

Pure data-driven models risk generating unphysical predictions (e.g. dust spontaneously generating without wind). PINN embeds physical prior laws directly into the objective function:

∂C/∂t + ∇ • (u C) = K ∇²C + S(u* − u*t) − D_dry − D_wet

Penalizes violations of mass continuity and ensures dust initiation only occurs when friction velocity exceeds aerodynamic saltation thresholds ($u_* > u_{*t}$).

🕸️ Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (GNN)

Atmospheric dust transport is fundamentally a topological spatial flow governed by pressure gradients and prevailing wind corridors.

GNN constructs nodes representing meteorological stations and desert grid points, with dynamic edge weights determined by 10m wind direction and 500hPa geopotential height differences, modeling dust propagation along the Hexi Corridor and Qinling mountain gaps.

⌛ Spatio-Temporal Transformers & Memory Networks

Unlike short-term nowcasting, extended-range (3–15 days) forecasting demands models that capture low-frequency climate variations and sub-seasonal memory.

Employs architectures derived from PredRNN (Wang 2017 [22]) and Earthformer (Gao 2022 [24]) with cuboid self-attention, isolating slow-varying soil moisture anomalies and upstream desert accumulation.

🤖 Automated Machine Learning (AutoGluon & AutoFeat)

Proven by Song (2024) [25, 26] to achieve a 96.72% hit rate in severe convective gale prediction. Automates deep feature synthesis and multi-layer model stacking, drastically reducing engineering cycle time.

💡 Explainable AI (SHAP & Attention Weight Mapping)

Unpacks the "black box" of deep learning. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values quantitatively attribute each forecast decision to specific physical drivers (e.g. 42% friction velocity, 31% soil moisture deficit, 27% upper-level trough).

Statistical Methodology

Statistical Formulations & Meteorological Verification

Rigorous mathematical standards used for class imbalance mitigation, probability calibration, and multi-dimensional forecast verification.

1. Critical Success Index (CSI / Threat Score)

CSI = Hits / (Hits + FalseAlarms + Misses)

The gold standard categorical metric in meteorological disaster verification. Penalizes both missed sandstorm events and unwarranted false alarms.

2. Probability of Detection (POD / Hit Rate)

POD = Hits / (Hits + Misses)

Measures the proportion of actual dust storm occurrences that were successfully forecast in advance. Critical for safeguarding lives and public health.

3. False Alarm Ratio (FAR)

FAR = FalseAlarms / (Hits + FalseAlarms)

Measures the fraction of issued warnings that did not materialize. Minimizing FAR prevents "warning fatigue" and unnecessary economic disruption.

4. SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling)

x_synth = x_i + λ • (x_neighbor − x_i), λ ∈ [0, 1]

Synthesizes realistic minority class feature vectors in high-dimensional space, overcoming severe historical sample imbalance where calm days exceed sandstorm days by 50:1.

5. Cost-Sensitive Loss Function

L_cost = − [ w_pos • y • log(p) + w_neg • (1 − y) • log(1 − p) ]

Assigns $w_{pos} \gg w_{neg}$ (e.g. 10:1 ratio) to penalize missed disasters significantly harder than false alarms during model backpropagation.

6. Quantile Regression (EALSTM-QR)

min_θ Σ ρ_τ (y − f_τ(x)), ρ_τ(u) = u • (τ − I(u < 0))

Generates non-parametric prediction intervals (τ = 0.05, 0.50, 0.95), providing decision-makers with quantified uncertainty bounds for PM10 dust concentrations.

Observation & Simulation Data

Multi-Modal Environmental Data Architecture

Four primary data modalities fused across spatial, temporal, and atmospheric dimensions.

1. Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP)

Providers: ECMWF, CMA, NASA GEOS-CF Ensemble

Parameters: 500hPa & 850hPa geopotential height, zonal ($u$) and meridional ($v$) wind velocity vectors, surface temperature ($T_{2m}$), mean sea level pressure (MSLP), boundary layer height (PBLH).

Spatiotemporal Spec: 0.125°–0.25° grid, 3h–6h intervals, 3–15 days lead time.

2. Atmospheric Climate Reanalysis

Providers: ECMWF ERA5 Reanalysis, NCEP/NCAR

Parameters: 1979–Present continuous atmospheric state, sub-seasonal indices (ENSO, Arctic Oscillation), multi-depth volumetric soil water (0–7cm, 7–28cm, 28–100cm), soil temperature layers.

Spatiotemporal Spec: Hourly/daily, 0.25° grid, 45-year continuous climate history.

3. Satellite Remote Sensing

Platforms: NASA MODIS (Terra/Aqua), Himawari-8/9, FY-4A/B (CMA)

Parameters: Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) [Rouse 1974 [14]], bare soil fraction, Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD at 550nm), Dust Storm Index (DSI), snow cover extent.

Spatiotemporal Spec: 250m–1km spatial resolution, 10-minute geostationary scanning cadence.

4. Surface In-Situ Observations

Networks: 2,400+ CMA national weather stations, CNEMC air quality network

Parameters: 10m surface wind speed and gusts, friction velocity $u_*$, horizontal visibility (meters), PM10 and PM2.5 concentrations (μg/m³), verified sandstorm event logs.

Spatiotemporal Spec: 10-minute / hourly continuous ground observations.
